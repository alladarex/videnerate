The word 'example' most commonly refers to a specific instance used to illustrate a general idea, rule, or concept. When people say "for example," they are introducing a concrete case that makes an abstract point easier to understand. In everyday life, an example might be a sample math problem in a textbook, a model behavior in a classroom, or a case study in a business meeting. The most popular use of the term, however, is in teaching and explanation, where an example serves as a bridge between theory and reality. An interesting fact about the idea of an example is that the ancient Greek philosopher Aristotle considered examples a powerful form of rhetorical proof, even more persuasive than logical arguments in some situations because people connect more easily with stories and specific instances than with abstract reasoning. This is why teachers, speakers, and writers rely on examples to make their points stick. Without examples, many complex ideas would remain confusing and out of reach for most people.
